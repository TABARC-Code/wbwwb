# Core design boundary

## What must remain true

The camera is the verb. The player chooses a fragment of the square, the
broadcast gives that fragment meaning, people react to the broadcast, and that
reaction produces the next photograph. Every extension has to return to that
loop quickly enough for the player to see cause and effect with their own eyes.

The game is not saying that one wicked reporter hypnotises an innocent crowd.
Selection, framing, repetition, audience appetite, social imitation and the
reward for spectacle all feed one another. A report can begin as distortion,
change how people behave, and then produce real evidence that appears to prove
the original distortion. That vicious little miracle remains the centre.

## What I am adding

New stories can make the system less predetermined without pretending every
choice has a clean answer. The player may exploit attention, sell symbols to
opposing groups, amplify a faction, refuse a profitable frame, or spend earned
money on a small repair. Those actions matter only if they alter visible people
and create new reportable behaviour. Numbers support that feedback. They do not
replace it.

Different people remember different sources, trust different outlets and pass
weaker versions of a message to neighbours. Influencers can turn the player
into content. Seasonal pressure can make one story more receptive than another.
None of this should require a lecture before the first photograph.

## What I am not building

There will be no sandbox mode. The authored sequence is part of the argument,
not an inconvenience to be removed. There will also be no sprawling newsroom
dashboard, universal morality score, or ninety-minute campaign quietly hiding
inside a five-minute satire.

The original hat, lovers, fear and violence sequence keeps its uninterrupted
camera-to-crowd rhythm. The agency strip appears only for new structured stories
where money, influence or intervention are actually in play. If a new feature
cannot explain itself through a changed headline, body, crowd or subsequent
photograph, it probably belongs in the notes rather than the game.

## Test for future ideas

Before adding a system, I ask four blunt questions:

1. Does the player cause it through framing, attention or intervention?
2. Can its effect be seen in the square rather than only in a counter?
3. Does it feed another photograph or alter how one will be interpreted?
4. Does it sharpen the feedback-loop argument without drowning the joke?

Four yes answers do not guarantee a good feature. Fewer than three usually
means I have designed a different game and left it wearing this one's hat.
